# Body mass

## Why it matters

Body mass is how much you weigh. Losing mass in flight usually means you are
eating less than you need, and it makes muscle, bone and heart loss worse.
https://nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/human-adaptation-2020-final.pdf

## How spaceflight affects it

A normal scale does not work without gravity. Your mass is measured with the
SLAMMD device, which applies a known force and measures how you accelerate.
Crews often lose mass slowly over a mission.
https://nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/human-adaptation-2020-final.pdf

## Measurement steps

1. Measure on the same day of the week.
2. Use the SLAMMD as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
3. Wear the same clothes each time.
4. Write the number in HeMoSo.

## Status and next steps

### no_data

HeMoSo needs 3 measurements before it can compare you with yourself.

Measure again next week on the same day. No one is notified for now.

### in_range

Your body mass today is within your usual range.

No action today. Measure again next week on the same day.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_range

Your body mass is below your usual range.

Eat your full meals this week and measure again next week. Assumption.

Your CMO or flight surgeon confirms.

### watch

Your body mass is below 70 kg, the line the crew uses.

Eat your full meals this week and measure again next week. The crew checks your
record on board.

## How to prevent problems

Eat enough energy and protein every day. Do the exercise your CMO schedules.
Keep the same weekly measure day. Assumption.
