# Sleep hours

## Why it matters

Sleep is when your body and brain recover. Crews sleep about 6 hours a night in
flight, less than on Earth. Weeks of short sleep lower attention, mood and how
the body uses energy.

## How spaceflight affects it

Crews sleep less in flight, and their body clock shifts. Work overload and the
schedule add to it. Light is the main signal for your body clock. You can use it
on purpose.

## Measurement steps

1. Note the time you went to bed.
2. Note the time you woke up.
3. Count the hours you slept, not the hours in bed. Assumption.
4. Write the number every morning.
5. Do it before you start work.

## Status and next steps

You slept in your usual range last night.

No action today. Keep the same sleep and wake times.

No one is notified for now.

### watch

You slept less than your usual range for 3 nights.

Go to bed at your usual time today. Ask your CMO about light and rest. Assumption.

Your CMO checks your record on board.

### consult

You have slept much less than your usual range for several nights.

Tell your CMO today. Keep the rest of your day light. Assumption.

Your CMO checks you in person.

### urgent

You have slept very little for several nights and you feel it in your work.

Tell your CMO now. Stop your tasks for today. Assumption.

Your CMO contacts the flight surgeon at once.

## How to prevent problems

Keep the same sleep and wake times every day. Use the scheduled rest. Ask your
CMO about light in your module. Assumption.
