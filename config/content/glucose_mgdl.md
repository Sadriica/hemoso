# Blood glucose

## Why it matters

Blood glucose is the sugar your body uses for energy. Crews often eat less than
they need in flight, and diet, sleep and stress change how the body handles
sugar. The challenge statement names blood sugar as an indicator to monitor.

## How spaceflight affects it

The NASA nutrition report says the body handles sugar differently in flight, and
that crews are thought to need about the same carbohydrates as on Earth. Studies
are few and do not agree. How blood sugar is measured on board is still open in
this project. Assumption.

## Measurement steps

1. Wash your hands.
2. Let them dry.
3. Use the meter as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
4. Measure before a meal. Assumption.
5. Write the number with the time.

## Status and next steps

### no_data

HeMoSo needs 3 entries before it can compare you with yourself.

Measure again at the same time tomorrow. No one is notified for now.

### in_range

Your blood glucose is in your usual range today.

No action today. Measure again at the same time tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_range

Your blood glucose is outside your usual range today.

Eat your next meal at the usual time and measure again before it. Assumption.

No one is notified for now.

### consult

Your blood glucose is far outside your usual range.

Tell your CMO today. Drink fluids and measure again in 2 hours. Assumption.

Your CMO checks you in person.

## How to prevent problems

Eat meals at regular times. Drink fluids. Tell your CMO if you feel very tired,
dizzy or thirsty. Assumption.
