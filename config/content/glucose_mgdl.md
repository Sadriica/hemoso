# Blood glucose

## Why it matters

Blood glucose is the sugar your body uses for energy. Crews often eat less than
they need in flight, and diet, sleep and stress change how the body handles
sugar. The challenge statement names blood sugar as an indicator to monitor.

## How spaceflight affects it

The nutrition evidence report describes changes in glucose metabolism in flight
and states that carbohydrate needs are thought to be similar to those on Earth.
Studies are few and do not agree, and how blood sugar is measured on board is
still open in this project. Assumption.

## Measurement steps

1. Wash your hands and let them dry.
2. Use the meter as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
3. Measure before a meal. Assumption.
4. Write the number with the time.

## Status and next steps

### at_baseline

Your blood glucose is at your baseline today.

No action today. Measure again at the same time tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_baseline

Your blood glucose has been above your baseline for 2 days.

Eat your next meal at the usual time and measure again before it. Assumption.

Your CMO is notified on board.

### needs_flight_surgeon

Your blood glucose is far from your baseline and it has stayed there.

Tell your CMO today. Drink fluids and measure again in 2 hours. Assumption.

Your CMO tells the flight surgeon at the next link with Earth.

## How to prevent problems

Eat regular meals and drink fluids. Tell your CMO if you feel very tired, dizzy
or thirsty. Assumption.
