# Diastolic blood pressure

## Why it matters

Diastolic blood pressure is the bottom number of your blood pressure. It shows
the pressure in your arteries between beats. Together with the top number it
tells your CMO how your circulation is coping with spaceflight.

## How spaceflight affects it

Without gravity, fluids move up to your head and chest and your blood vessels
change. On return to gravity the pressure can drop, and some astronauts faint.
Heat, sleep loss and low fluid intake also change the reading.

## Measurement steps

1. Sit still for 5 minutes.
2. Put the cuff on your arm as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
3. Keep your arm at the level of your heart.
4. Measure once. Assumption.
5. Wait one minute.
6. Measure again. Assumption.
7. Write the bottom number.

## Status and next steps

### no_data

HeMoSo needs 3 entries before it can compare you with yourself.

Measure again at the same hour. No one is notified for now.

### in_range

Your diastolic blood pressure is in your usual range today.

No action today. Measure again at the same hour tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_range

Your diastolic blood pressure is outside your usual range today.

Rest 10 minutes and measure again. Keep the record.

No one is notified for now.

### consult

Your diastolic blood pressure is far outside your usual range.

Sit down and tell your CMO today. Drink fluids and measure again in 1 hour.
Assumption.

Your CMO checks you in person.

### urgent

Your diastolic blood pressure is well above your usual range.

Sit down now and call your CMO. Do not exercise. Assumption.

Your CMO contacts the flight surgeon at once.

## How to prevent problems

Exercise every day. Eat well. Drink fluids before landing. Wear the lower-body
compression garment when your CMO asks. Assumption.
