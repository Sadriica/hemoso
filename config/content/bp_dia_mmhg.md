# Diastolic blood pressure

## Why it matters

Diastolic blood pressure is the bottom number of your blood pressure. It shows
the pressure in your arteries between beats. Together with the top number it
tells your CMO how your circulation is coping with spaceflight.

## How spaceflight affects it

Without gravity, fluids move up to your head and chest and your blood vessels
change. On return to gravity the pressure can drop, and some astronauts faint.
Heat, sleep loss and low fluid intake also change the reading.

## Measurement steps

1. Sit still for 5 minutes.
2. Put the cuff on your arm as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
3. Keep your arm at the level of your heart.
4. Measure twice, one minute apart. Assumption.
5. Write the second number.

## Status and next steps

### at_baseline

Your diastolic blood pressure is at your baseline today.

No action today. Measure again at the same hour tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_baseline

Your diastolic blood pressure has been below your baseline for 3 days.

Rest 10 minutes and measure again. Check your fluid intake and how you slept
last night. Assumption.

Your CMO is notified on board.

### needs_flight_surgeon

Your diastolic blood pressure is far below your baseline.

Sit down and tell your CMO today. Drink fluids and measure again in 1 hour.
Assumption.

Your CMO tells the flight surgeon at the next link with Earth.

## How to prevent problems

Exercise every day and eat well. Drink fluids before landing and wear the
lower-body compression garment when your CMO asks. Assumption.
