# Fluid intake

## Why it matters

Water keeps your blood volume and blood pressure stable. In flight, drinking
less than you need is common, and dehydration makes you slower and less
accurate. https://nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/human-adaptation-2020-final.pdf

## How spaceflight affects it

Fluids move to your head and chest in the first days, and the body reduces its
water. A busy schedule and food that needs water to rehydrate can lower how
much you drink. https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/

## Measurement steps

1. Fill your bottle at the galley and note the amount.
2. Count the water you drink and the water you use for food.
3. Add both at the end of the day.
4. Write the total in HeMoSo by hand.

## Status and next steps

### no_data

HeMoSo needs 3 days before it can compare you with yourself.

Log your total again tomorrow. No one is notified for now.

### in_range

Your fluid intake today is within your usual range.

No action today. Log your total again tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_range

Your fluid intake today is below your usual range.

Drink a glass of water now and log your total again tomorrow. Assumption.

Your CMO or flight surgeon confirms.

### watch

Your fluid intake is below 1500 mL today, well under the 2.5 L a day NASA asks
for.

Drink some fluid now and log your total again tomorrow.

The crew checks your record on board.

## How to prevent problems

Drink at regular times, not only when you are thirsty. Drink before exercise.
Tell your CMO if your urine looks dark. Assumption.
