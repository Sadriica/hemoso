# Appetite

## Why it matters

Appetite is how much you feel like eating. Crews often eat less than they need,
and appetite changes are common in flight: in one Mir study, 40% of crew
members reported less appetite. https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-013-food-and-nutrition.pdf

## How spaceflight affects it

Fluids move to your head, the schedule is tight and food tastes different.
Those changes can lower appetite, and eating less than you need adds up over
weeks. https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Evidence/other/Nutrition%20ER_human_adaptation_2020_final.pdf

## Measurement steps

1. Answer at the same time every day, before the evening meal.
2. Rate your appetite from 1 to 5.
3. 1 means no appetite and 5 means very hungry.
4. Write the number in HeMoSo.

## Status and next steps

### no_data

HeMoSo needs 3 answers before it can compare you with yourself.

Answer again tomorrow at the same time. No one is notified for now.

### in_range

Your appetite today is within your usual range.

No action today. Answer again tomorrow at the same time.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_range

Your appetite today is lower than your usual range.

Eat something small at the next meal time and answer again tomorrow.
Assumption.

Your CMO or flight surgeon confirms.

## How to prevent problems

Eat at regular times. Eat with the crew when you can. Tell your CMO if food
tastes different for several days. Assumption.
