# Core body temperature

## Why it matters

Your core temperature is the temperature inside your chest. In space it rises
about 1 °C and stays higher after 2 to 3 months. This mild fever costs about 20%
more energy, so it matters for how you eat, sleep and perform.

## How spaceflight affects it

There is no moving air in weightlessness to carry heat away, so heat builds up,
and crews report feeling hot during exercise. Your in-flight baseline is about
1 °C above your Earth baseline, and it comes back down slowly after landing. A
temperature far above your own baseline can also be the first sign of an
infection. Assumption.

## Measurement steps

1. Measure at the same hour every day. Your temperature is lowest between 4 and
   6 in the morning.
2. Put the sensor on your forehead as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
3. Sit still for 5 minutes.
4. Write the number.

## Status and next steps

### at_baseline

Your temperature is at your baseline today.

No action today. Measure again at the same hour tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_baseline

Your temperature has been above your baseline for 2 days.

Measure again in 4 hours. Check your fluid intake and how you slept. Assumption.

Your CMO is notified on board.

### needs_flight_surgeon

Your temperature is far above your baseline and it has stayed high.

Tell your CMO today. Rest, drink fluids and measure again in 2 hours.
Assumption.

Your CMO tells the flight surgeon at the next link with Earth.

## How to prevent problems

Keep your sleep schedule and drink fluids. Exercise when your CMO schedules it.
Assumption.
