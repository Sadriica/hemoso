# Core body temperature

## Why it matters

Your core temperature is the temperature inside your chest. In space it rises
about 1 °C and stays higher after 2 to 3 months. This mild fever costs about 20%
more energy, so it matters for how you eat, sleep and perform.

## How spaceflight affects it

In space there is no moving air to carry heat away, so heat builds up. Crews
report feeling hot during exercise. Your usual range in space is about 1 °C
higher than on Earth, and it comes back down slowly after landing. A temperature
far above your own usual range can also be the first sign of an infection.
Assumption.

## Measurement steps

1. Measure at the same hour every day. Your temperature is lowest between 4 and
   6 in the morning.
2. Put the sensor on your forehead as your CMO showed you. Assumption.
3. Sit still for 5 minutes.
4. Write the number.

## Status and next steps

### no_data

HeMoSo needs 3 entries before it can compare you with yourself.

Measure again at the same hour. No one is notified for now.

### in_range

Your temperature is in your usual range today.

No action today. Measure again at the same hour tomorrow.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_range

Your temperature is outside your usual range today.

Rest 10 minutes and measure again. Keep the record.

No one is notified for now.

### consult

Your temperature is far outside your usual range. A reading of 38.0 °C or more
also counts as above the fever line; your own usual range comes first.

Tell your CMO today. Rest, drink fluids and measure again in 2 hours.
Assumption.

Your CMO checks you in person.

### urgent

Your temperature is well above your usual range, or well below it.

Tell your CMO now. Rest and drink fluids. Assumption.

Your CMO contacts the flight surgeon at once.

## How to prevent problems

Keep your sleep schedule. Drink fluids. Exercise when your CMO schedules it.
Assumption.
