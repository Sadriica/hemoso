# Mood

Answer this before you sleep, every day.

The scale: 1 very low, 2 low, 3 neutral, 4 good, 5 very good.

## Why it matters

A change in mood is an early sign of a mental health problem. Isolation,
confinement and distance from Earth wear it down over weeks, and NASA uses
questionnaires and journaling to catch the change early, while it is still easy
to treat.

## How spaceflight affects it

Isolation and confinement change how people feel, and sleep loss and work
overload add to it. One bad day is normal. A change that lasts days or weeks is
what your CMO needs to see.

## Measurement steps

1. Answer before you sleep.
2. Pick the number that fits today: 1 very low, 2 low, 3 neutral, 4 good, 5 very
   good.
3. Write one line about what changed today, if anything.
4. Answer every day, also when you feel fine. The pattern is what matters.

## Status and next steps

### at_baseline

Your mood is at your baseline this week.

No action today. Answer again before you sleep.

No one is notified for now.

### outside_baseline

Your mood has been below your baseline for a week.

Write two lines about your day. Talk to someone on the crew today. Assumption.

Your CMO is notified on board.

### needs_flight_surgeon

Your mood has stayed below your baseline for several days.

Tell your CMO today. Keep your sleep schedule and talk to your family at the next
link. Assumption.

Your CMO tells the flight surgeon at the next link with Earth.

## How to prevent problems

Keep your sleep schedule. Write in your journal. Keep contact with your family.
Talk to the crew. Use the window and the plants in your module. Assumption.
