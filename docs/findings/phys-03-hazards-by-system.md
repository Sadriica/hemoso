**Owner:** phys

## Claim

Each of the five systems the app shows is linked by NASA to at least one of the
five spaceflight hazards, with a stated mechanism, and none of those pages gives a
value at which the effect becomes a problem.

## The official risk page behind each system

The risks index links 17 pages. These three were opened on Sat 14 and add a concrete
detail that the hazard pages do not have.

| System | Risk page | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sleep | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-from-inadequate-sleep-and-irregular-schedules/ | Crew witness "16 sunrises and sunsets per day"; LEDs simulate Earth's light-dark cycle; light and caffeine use affect the biological clock; relaxation time is scheduled to prevent burnout |
| Neuro-behavioural | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-behavioral-conditions-and-psychiatric-disorders/ | Isolation and confinement raise the risk of anxiety and depression, which affect "sleep, morale, and decision making"; journaling, plants, windows and privacy are the named countermeasures |
| Immune (context) | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-altered-immune-system-responses/ | "Radiation, microgravity, poor sleep and other spaceflight stressors can alter the immune system"; crews report cold sores, rashes, atypical allergies and infections |

The other risk pages are listed on https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/risks/ and were not
opened. Their one-line summaries are on that index and can be cited from it, not from
the risk page itself.

## What prevents it (question 5 of the statement)

The hazard pages say what changes. These pages say what NASA does about it.

| System | Countermeasure | The number behind it | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cardiovascular and musculoskeletal | Resistance and aerobic exercise, plus periodic fitness self-evaluations | **1.5 h per day, 6 to 7 days a week**; average losses are still **10 % to 12 %** of pre-mission fitness, and **12 % to 38 %** of crew lose more than 20 % | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-reduced-cardiorespiratory-and-musculoskeletal-fitness/ |
| Cardiovascular and musculoskeletal | Even with exercise, some crew do not meet weightlessness fitness standards, and many would not meet standards for walking on a surface; many have not recovered by the end of the 30-day reconditioning | Individual variability "cannot be fully explained by countermeasure adherence" | https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks/risk.aspx?i=180 |
| Sleep | LEDs simulate the Earth light-dark cycle; relaxation time is scheduled; light and caffeine use are studied as clock inputs | Crew see **16 sunrises and sunsets per day** | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-from-inadequate-sleep-and-irregular-schedules/ |
| Neuro-behavioural | Journaling, plants, windows, privacy, and contact with family and clinicians | Windows reduce monotony and the sense of confinement | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-behavioral-conditions-and-psychiatric-disorders/ |
| Immune | Blood, saliva and urine monitoring; samples returned to Earth for analysis | Symptoms reported on orbit: cold sores, rashes, atypical allergies, infections | https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-altered-immune-system-responses/ |
| Bone | Resistive exercise; the programme limit is 95 % of pre-mission density for hip and spine | Loss of **1 % to 1.5 % per month** without countermeasures | https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-030-bone-loss.pdf |

The point for the app: exercise is prescribed and measured, and it still does not fully
work. An app that says "exercise more" without showing the prescribed dose is telling
the crew less than NASA already tells them.

## Evidence, and why the immune risk page matters
The HRP risk pages connect a body system with the stressors behind it. The immune
risk names them explicitly in its gap IM-106: "the effect of the stressors associated
with spaceflight (stress, microgravity, radiation, altered nutrition, circadian
misalignment, hypoxia, etc.) on human immunity".

That sentence is the closest NASA comes to naming the hazards together, and it is
why the table above can be defended: the mechanism is stated, the threshold is not.

| Source | What it says (short quote) | Type | Link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Risk of altered immune response, HSRB state of knowledge | "Subclinical altered human immune function has been documented in crewmembers post-flight & in-flight … These immune changes persist for the duration of a 6 month orbital flight" | NASA | https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks/risk.aspx?i=85 |
| Risk of altered immune response, gap IM-106 | The stressors are named: "stress, microgravity, radiation, altered nutrition, circadian misalignment, hypoxia" | NASA | https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Risks/risk.aspx?i=85 |
| Hazard: Gravity Fields | "Water and other fluids in the body shift upward to the head, which may put pressure on the eyes and cause vision problems" | NASA | https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-gravity-fields/ |
| Hazard: Isolation and Confinement | "Sleep loss, circadian desynchronization, and work overload compound this issue" | NASA | https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-isolation-and-confinement/ |

## Confidence

High for the links themselves: each row quotes a NASA page that states the
mechanism in one sentence. Medium for the cardiovascular `radiation` row, because the
0.5 Gy-Eq figure comes from the evidence report page quoted in
`team/eng1/notes/challenge-resources.md` and has not been reopened by Phys.

## Limits and open questions

- It says what changes, not when it becomes a problem. Question 4 stays unanswered
  here and belongs to `config/rules/attention.csv` (Bio 2).
- The immune system is affected and has no indicator in the app. It appears only as
  context, and that is a deliberate omission, not an oversight.
- The metabolic rows are the weakest: nutrition is not one of the five hazards, so the
  link is made through body mass and diet, not directly.
